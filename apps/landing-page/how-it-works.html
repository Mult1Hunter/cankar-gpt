In plain words

How it works ❧

No jargon required. What a language model actually is, how the famous ones are built, and how this small Slovene one was made differently.

What is a language model?

A language model is a program that has learned the patterns of a language by reading a great deal of text. Give it the start of a sentence and it predicts which word is likely to come next, then the next, and so on. That is really all "generating text" is: very good next-word guessing, one small piece at a time.

It has no database of facts and no understanding in the human sense. It has absorbed the statistical habits of a language - which words tend to follow which - and replays them.

Think of someone who has read enormously and can finish your sentences in a plausible way, without necessarily knowing whether what they are saying is true.

How language models are usually built

The famous models - the ones behind the popular chat assistants - are built at enormous scale, in roughly three moves:

1. Gather text

Hundreds of billions, even trillions, of words scraped from the web, books, and code.

2. Learn the pieces

The text is chopped into small sub-word units (called tokens) that the model works with instead of whole words.

3. Pretraining

The model reads all that text over and over, nudging billions of internal numbers (its parameters) until it predicts the next piece well. This runs on thousands of specialized chips for weeks and can cost millions of dollars. A final polishing step then makes the model pleasant and safe to chat with.

The result is powerful and general - but expensive, mostly closed, and English-first. Slovene is a rounding error in that training data.

How CankarGPT is built

This project uses the same core idea - learn a language by predicting the next piece - but deliberately tiny, cheap, and from scratch, in Slovene:

  • The text is public-domain Slovene literature plus Slovenian Wikipedia (about 73 million words) - not the whole web.
  • The tokenizer is trained from scratch on Slovene, so the pieces fit Slovene words, not English ones.
  • The model has 26 million parameters - thousands of times smaller than frontier models.
  • Pretraining ran on one desktop graphics card in under half an hour, for essentially no money.
  • A short specialization pass then taught it the prose voice of one author, Ivan Cankar.

Because it is small, it does not reason or hold facts well - but it convincingly learns a voice. The next stage turns it into a styler: you write plain modern Slovene, and it rewrites the same meaning in Cankar's register.

A frontier model is a vast general library staffed by thousands. CankarGPT is a single apprentice who studied one author closely - not encyclopedic, but with a recognizable hand.

Why build it the small way?

  • To understand the whole pipeline by building every piece yourself, not by calling someone else's API.
  • To show that meaningful, measurable results are possible on a hobby budget.
  • To give Slovene - and one of its greatest prose writers - a small model of its own.

Everything here is measured against a frozen test set, open-source, and public from the first commit. If a claim does not carry a number, it does not go on the page.
